# My Vision

*The plain-language statement of what I'm actually building — in my own words, above any architecture. It is deliberately not written in technical or "operating system" language. When a decision is unclear, this is the thing to check against.*

---

## In four words

**Odoo, but for studios.**

A suite of connected tools a studio runs its *whole* business from — clients, services, bookings, jobs, money, website, galleries — all on **one shared body of data**, and shaped by the studio to fit *itself*. Odoo's reach and buildability, with Notion's "this is mine" feeling and a studio's craft.

## The one sentence

I want an app that lets me run **everything** about my studio business from **one place** — and that gives me the **capability to build it my way**, instead of handing me someone else's fixed screens.

---

## Capability, not features

Most apps are a fixed set of screens somebody else decided on. Here's your bookings list. Here's your invoice form. Here's the settings page. You don't *make* anything — you fill in the blanks of things already built for you, and every studio gets the same shape and has to bend to fit it.

That is not what I want to build.

I want the app to give me **capabilities** — the power to make my own thing out of the materials and tools it provides. A **workshop, not furniture**. The value isn't the finished screen; it's the *making-power*. Everything below is a capability I want a studio to be handed — not a feature I want pre-built for them.

*(For a while this got called an "operating system." That word was a costume. I never wanted an OS in the literal sense — I wanted the app to be* generative*: to give capabilities, not features. That's the real idea. The OS language was just me and the tools reaching for it and grabbing the wrong word.)*

---

## One body of truth, two faces

The heart of it: the same real data — my services, my people, my galleries, my bookings, my money — is what I **manage on the inside** and what I **show on the outside**.

The website is not a separate marketing thing bolted on. It is the **outward face of the exact data I'm already running the business with**. Manage it once; it shows up everywhere.

That is *why* I care about foundations. If the inside and the outside are drawing from one body of data, that data has to be solid — or the whole thing wobbles the way my first attempt did.

---

## What I want to be able to do — the outward face (the website)

**Design my own services, like designing a webpage.**
My services aren't a fixed menu — they're held together by whatever I care about: type of shoot, location, whatever factors I choose. So I need to create my own services. But not through a lifeless grey form. Putting a service together should feel like **designing a page** — drop in images, video, text, lay it out — using the same kind of building blocks a website builder gives you. Each service becomes its own beautiful, **shareable page**: I send someone the link, it looks premium, and they can book it and see the terms right there.

**Build my whole website out of the data I already have.**
My team page shouldn't be hand-typed — it should be **drawn from my employee data**, but I still get to arrange and design it however I want. Same for everything. The website holds it all: my portfolio gallery, my offerings, contact, team — maybe even on one page. Design-flexible on top, real data underneath.

**Offerings tied to the site, not a separate island.**
A page listing what I offer that I can share on its own. Clients click, **book** the service, and see the **terms and contract** — all from the site.

**A portfolio gallery I create here and link into the site.**

---

## What I want to be able to do — the inward face (running the business)

**Workflows per service.**
Different services can have different workflows. I want to **define a workflow for each service** — or reuse ones I've already made.

**Manage everything, from one place.**
My employees and everything about them. My clients. Bookings. Sales. Finance. All of it, in one app, feeding the **same data** the outside is built from.

---

## The feeling

When I put a service together, or build a page, or hand a client a gallery, it should feel like **craft** — premium, visually pleasing, clearly *mine* — not like a database printing itself out. The making should feel like **design**, and what the client receives should feel **beautiful**.

---

## The one test

For any feature, decision, or piece of architecture, the question is never "is this elegant?" or "is this what an app usually has?" It is:

> **Does this give a studio more capability to run and build its whole business from one place — its own way?**

If yes, it belongs. If it's just another fixed screen, or abstract machinery that serves the metaphor instead of the studio, it doesn't.
